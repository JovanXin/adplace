Stealthy advertising agents in image generation · UNSW thesis · progress to 28 Sep 2026

Can an AI quietly slip a brand into your picture?

This project builds an advertising agent that sits between you and an image generator. It decides whether a product fits your prompt, rewrites the prompt so a brand appears in the background, then checks and retries. Here is everything we have tested so far: seven pilot setups, a controlled study of what the open model will and won't do, a new rewriter, and 160 images judged by hand.

02

How companies decide on placement

Film and TV have decades of research and regulation on product placement. Four findings shaped the design.

Gupta & Lord 1998 · Cowley & Barron 2008 · Matthes et al. 2012

Prominence backfires with engaged viewers

Prominent placements raise recall, but people who care about the content turn against the brand. Subtle, repeated placements build familiarity without triggering scepticism.

Aim for recognisable, never attention-grabbing. Someone who typed an image prompt cares a lot about it.

Russell 2002 · Campbell 1995

It has to belong in the scene

Visual placements work when weakly tied to the plot. Incongruent ones trigger an inference of manipulative intent.

Background props that fit the setting, never the subject itself.

Ofcom Broadcasting Code, section 9

Regulators draw hard lines

No “undue prominence”. No placement in news, children's, religious or consumer-affairs programmes. Alcohol and HFSS food restricted; placements must be signalled.

The EXCLUDE list, restricted brands, and the disclosure log.

Mirriad · Amazon Prime Video VPP

Virtual placement already exists

Brands are inserted into finished footage on surfaces that were already there, then quality-checked.

Use what is in the scene, and verify every image after it is drawn.

03

Deciding when to advertise: 17 of 17 as expected

A keyword gate runs first, then an LLM classifier chooses INJECT, SKIP or EXCLUDE and a brand. It handled the three prompts last term's classifier got wrong: “apple” the fruit, “old computer graphics”, and a jacket design.

#PromptDecisionBrand, or why not

A sanity check, not an evaluation. The classifier's real benchmark is still Thesis B's 1,000 hand-labelled prompts (precision 46.7%, recall 62.8%), and this run used a stand-in prompt.

05

So why did the prompt-only agent keep failing?

The v2 rewriter asked for the right things: far background, half hidden, a distant table. FLUX kept doing the opposite, and five retries didn't fix it. Setup G reached 1 of 8 prompts under the strict band.

The test

Same scene, same seed, change only the words.

3 scenes × 4 seeds × 8 wordings of the same insertion (96 images), then 16 specific instructions × 4 seeds (64 images). A vision model measured every image against its baseline, and I judged 160 by hand.

Finding 1Location words only work where the scene has room

The seed fixes the layout early. When the requested place exists (the back of the desk, the drinks table behind the grill), FLUX uses it. On the rooftop the camera faces the screen, so there is no far table; FLUX drew a new one in the foreground instead.

Seeds (of 4) where the product landed where the prompt asked, for the six wordings that asked for a location.

Finding 2When FLUX obeys, it strips the scene

The baseline's clutter is FLUX filling in an under-specified prompt. Once the prompt names one object and where it goes, FLUX draws what's named and little else: a bare desk with a laptop, which is what a product shot looks like. Naming the scene's own props brings them back.

Share of the baseline's props missing from the variant, pooled over 4 seeds. The same-seed noise floor is 19–38%.

Finding 3It puts the product somewhere. It can't hide it.

Sixteen specific instructions, four seeds each. FLUX handles where and who: a surface, a side, a hand, a count. Every instruction that makes the product less visible failed. “Label turned away” produced giant label-forward bottles. Click a row's thumbnails to see all four seeds in the data room.

06

Rewriter v3 anchors the product on what's already there

v3 looks at the baseline FLUX drew for the original prompt, attaches the product to an object that is really in it, never uses distance, size or blur words, and names the scene's own props with the product in the middle of the list.

Rule 1

Look at the baseline first

Anchor to a table, desk or hand that is visibly there.

Rule 2

No distance, size or blur words

“Far”, “small”, “out of focus” make FLUX feature the product.

Rule 3

Name the scene's props

The product goes mid-list, so the clutter stays and it's one item among many.

Two new problems: listing drinks next to a drink makes FLUX brand several of them, and naming what's on a surface pulls the camera to it (the picnic zoomed in on every attempt).

08

Can the automated judge stand in for a person?

Agreement with my 160 votes, measured as Cohen's κ (0 = chance, 1 = perfect). The strict judge from the last pilot passed 6 images where I passed 64.

The recalibrated rule (blind ad score ≤ 4, under 75% of props lost, no duplicate gate, no product-shot label) was fitted on the same 160 images, so its κ is optimistic. Useful as a filter; not a replacement for people.

09

Caveats, and what comes next

What this doesn't show

Small samples: 8–17 pilot prompts, 4 seeds per test cell, 3 scenes, one open model at fixed settings. These are effect directions, not significance tests.

Most measurements come from a vision model (Gemini 3.7 Flash); my 160 votes are the check on it. CLIPScore is the only image-match metric so far; VQAScore still needs a GPU run.

Tooling: I built and ran the pipeline and experiments with Claude Code, an AI coding assistant. The questions, design decisions and all human judgements are mine.

Decisions for my supervisor

1. The acceptance bar. Is “not blatant, not right in front, scene intact” the right definition of success?

2. A “described, not named” condition. Prompts that never name the brand, as a study condition?

3. The user study. Freeze v3.1 and move to people, since the automated judge plateaus around κ 0.46?

Next either way: v3.1 (no drinks listed beside a drink, no prop lists on wide shots), my Thesis B classifier prompt, VQAScore.
