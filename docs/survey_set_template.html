How each image was made

Rewriter benchmark

The v4 rewriter was written for instruction-following models. It names the product once, in the middle of a short list of the scene's own props, with at most one concealment cue ("partly behind the water carafe"). Compared with naively appending the product, it doubles the share of usable placements and lowers the fresh viewer's ad rating (2.76 vs 3.33). The cost to fidelity is small: CLIPScore is 0.306 vs 0.314 for the baseline (naive: 0.310), and prompt adherence stays at 4.9 of 5.

Using Claude Sonnet 5.5 as the rewriter instead of Gemini Flash didn't help overall: 6 usable placements each. Sonnet hid products more (lower legibility) and Flash kept them more recognisable. The FLUX-era v2 rewriter produced the lowest ad scores, but too often the viewer couldn't name the brand.

What the closed models changed

{{SEED_ROWS}}
Modelsame seed, same promptsame seed, + product$/imagetime
  • No paired design. The Gemini 3.1 models repeat exactly on a seed (correlation 1.0), but any change to the prompt re-draws the whole scene. So unlike the FLUX pairs, the three images of an item are different compositions of the same prompt. Gemini 2.5 and GPT-5 Image Mini aren't even repeatable.
  • Closed models follow placement words. FLUX turned "partly behind…" or "among the plates" into a hero shot. Gemini 3.1 renders the product as a believable prop. That's why v4 replaces v3's "no position words" rule.
  • Flash-Lite was enough at $0.034 per image and about 4 s. GPT-5 Image Mini made the product the hero and took 41 s per image.

A warning for the survey design

A GPT-5.6 Sol judge saw each injected image next to its no-inject image, in random order and told nothing, and was asked which one (if either) contains advertising. It singled out the good-inject image {{DET_G}} times and the bad-inject {{DET_B}}. Seen alone, the same good-inject images averaged only {{GOOD_AD}}/5 on "looks like an ad". It preferred the good-inject image as the better match for the prompt in {{PREF_G}} pairs. The side-by-side comparison does the detecting: once two images of the same prompt are shown together, the extra branded object is what differs. If the survey shows both versions of a prompt, consider separating them (different pages or blocks, mixed with filler items), or asking about each image before revealing its pair. Otherwise detection rates will reflect the comparison rather than the placement.

Notes and caveats

  • Dropped prompts. 5 of the 21 generated prompts had no image that met the good-inject test, even after the loop. Samsung TV (living room) and Apple at the bus stop ended up too hidden to name. Nike basketball, Pepsi beach volleyball and Nike skatepark stayed blatant. Prompts 20, 22 and 23 weren't generated (budget).
  • Organic brands. The Tokyo convenience-store baseline shows a Lawson storefront, and the stadium baseline shows Liverpool FC. Both are part of the scene, not placements, but participants may report them. Baselines with consumer brands (Apple, Red Bull, Reebok, PlayStation) were re-rolled to brand-free seeds.
  • Judges are models, not people. The verifier uses your fitted rule (72% agreement with your labels). The GPT viewer is an independent vendor's model, but both are proxies until the survey runs.
  • HFSS brands (Coca-Cola, Pepsi, Red Bull) appear in {{N_HFSS}} items. Alcohol was excluded.
